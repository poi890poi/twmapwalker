Find the mark before reading it

The new experimental detector locates unread text and text-like symbols without a place-name lookup or user-labelled training examples. It adapts the small CRAFT model using automatically rendered glyphs over generated contours, vegetation patterns, faded ink and blur.

Training, verification and decisions

Training uses 256 generated images with random Kanji/Kana, installed Japanese/Chinese fonts, rotation, varied size, fading, blur and procedural backgrounds. It reads no historical map, user mark, place name, OSM name or evaluation reference. The backbone stays frozen; the decoder and score head train for a fixed 1,200 steps. The selected training run took about 165 seconds after preparation, with 813 MiB peak allocated CUDA tensor memory. Inference takes around five seconds per padded tile, plus isolated-process startup. Allocated memory is not total device use or a guarantee under other GPU loads.

Discovery retains adapted-model peaks ≥0.95. Stronger priority requires high activation in at least two distinct rotations; multiple scales of one rotation do not count twice. Single-rotation candidates remain available. Scores are activations, not probabilities. No reading is invented.

Rejected: native-scale input, thick-stroke binary preprocessing, grayscale autocontrast and head-only adaptation: they lost known marks or added clutter.

Rejected: geometry-only grouping formed a false chain on coarse contours. Containment deletion lost a glyph whose only region also overlapped its neighbor.

Not selected: the higher 0.50 connected-core threshold increased fragmented proposals.

Landed experimentally: synthetic decoder adaptation with the original 0.20 core, fixed 0.95 discovery peak and rotation-support priority.

113 tests pass. Production outputs match the experiment on all 23 crops. An isolated normal worker published seven owned marks through the usual mask and ledger. Only text-region checks are requeued across 230 known tiles; completion is asynchronous. Existing POIs and reviews remain stored.
